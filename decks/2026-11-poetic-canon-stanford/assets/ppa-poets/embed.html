      How to read this map

      Each dot is a poet (a Wikidata identity) named in works of the Princeton Prosody Archive. The map shows co-invocation: which poets prosodists name in the same stretch of prose. It is not an influence map, and being named together does not mean affinity; a pairing can be a contrast, a list or a lineage.

      Links

      Two poets are linked when both are named within the same five-page window in at least three works, and in more works than you would expect from how often each is named overall. The excess can be large (Shakespeare and Milton share 1,281 works, about 171 more than expected) or tiny. Darker, thicker lines carry more excess. By default each poet shows only its three strongest links; switch to “All” to see all 33,573.

      Colour

      Community colours are the seven Gephi modularity communities shown in Figure 7 of the article, with the article's names for them. Community detection is randomised, so the boundaries are approximate: repeated runs gave six or seven groups. Period colours group poets by birth year + 20, as in the article's Figure 5.

      Size and position

      By default, size is the number of poets a poet is linked to (as in Figure 7), not how often the poet is named. Milton is named in more works than anyone, but has fewer distinctive partners than Wordsworth. Switch Size to “Works naming” to compare. Positions come from a force layout made once in Gephi: poets that share many partners sit close together. Distance is a rough guide only. A poet with a single link sits beside that one partner, whatever its period.

      Years

      “One window” keeps the archive-wide positions and shows a 30-year publication window (moved in 10-year steps). Grey dots are poets not named in that window. Hollow rings are poets named there but with no link attested in that window. A window's links are the archive-wide links that also pass the same test inside that window. More than half the archive's works date from 1870–1929, so the map itself mostly reflects that period. Windows with fewer than 50 works are hatched: read them as a list of names, not as structure.
